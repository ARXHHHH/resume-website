Read Piano Music

Learn a few landmark notes, then read everything else by counting steps from them. Ten minutes a day is enough.

Why it didn't stick before

Most people try to memorise every note as a separate fact, decoding each one with a rhyme like "Every Good Boy Does Fine". That's slow, and it falls apart once real music speeds up. Fluent readers work differently:

  • They know about 7 landmark notes cold. Everything else is "one step up from G" or "a skip down from middle C".
  • They read the shape. Notes going up on the page go right on the keyboard. A line note followed by the next space is a step (the next key). Line to line is a skip (jump one key).
  • They practise little and often. Ten minutes a day beats an hour on Sunday, because note reading is a recognition skill like learning a new alphabet.

How to use this page

  1. Work through the Lessons tab. Each lesson ends with exercises you pass by getting a streak right, fast. A lesson is done when every exercise is passed.
  2. Spend 5 minutes in the Note drill on whatever level the plan says.
  3. Clap one rhythm along with the metronome.
  4. Play a few bars of one song, saying the note names out loud.

Everything makes sound, so tap any key or note to hear it. Your phone's silent switch may mute it.

Rhythm: clap and count

Press Play. Count out loud with the clicks ("1, 2, 3, 4") and clap on every note. Rests are silent: keep counting but don't clap.

Speed

Read real music

Before you play, read it out loud. Say the note names in rhythm, then play slowly. Turn letters off once you can, so you're reading the music and not the labels.

When you get stuck

  • Can't name a note? Find the nearest landmark and count steps. Don't guess, and don't reach for the rhyme first.
  • Drill feels too hard? Drop back a level. Speed comes from easy wins repeated, not from struggle.
  • Missed a few days? Restart at the last level you could do 10 in a row. No catching up needed.
  • Bored? Pick any simple melody you love and work out the first 4 bars from sheet music. Real music is the best drill.
